# Daniell cell — what must be SEEN correctly

Written to the video-prompt skill's Step 0: only the things that appear on
screen. Every "never" below becomes an explicit ban in the NEGATIVE list, and
every shape/colour/direction becomes a check the frame review grades against.

NCERT Class 12 Chemistry (MP Board Ch. 2, विद्युत् रसायन) is the authority here,
not the question bank — see the GAG/GUG entry in the bug ledger for why that
distinction is load-bearing.

## The two half-cells

| | left half-cell | right half-cell |
|---|---|---|
| electrode | **zinc** — dull blue-grey, matte | **copper** — warm reddish-brown |
| solution | **zinc sulphate, ZnSO₄** | **copper sulphate, CuSO₄** |
| solution colour | **COLOURLESS**, like water | **BLUE** |
| role | **anode**, negative | **cathode**, positive |
| reaction | Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ (oxidation) | Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu (reduction) |
| what happens to the rod | **dissolves** — thins, pits, loses mass | **grows** — a rough dark layer deposits, gains mass |

Joined by a **salt bridge** (inverted U-tube) between the two solutions, and by a
**wire** between the two rods. E°cell = 1.10 V.

## Direction, and it is the most-failed fact

Electrons travel **zinc → copper through the external wire**. Never the reverse.
Inside the cell, current is carried by ions through the salt bridge; the two
solutions never touch each other directly.

## Order — what changes, and when

1. The circuit is completed.
2. Zinc begins to dissolve: the surface roughens and pits, and the rod thins.
3. Electrons move off through the wire.
4. Copper deposits on the copper rod as a dull, rough, dark reddish layer.
5. Over time the blue of the copper sulphate **pales**, because Cu²⁺ is being
   used up.

The zinc side stays colourless throughout. It does not turn blue at any point.

## COMMON ERRORS TO AVOID

Each of these has to be impossible to read into the clip:

- the zinc rod **growing, thickening or brightening** instead of dissolving
- the copper rod **dissolving or thinning** instead of growing
- **blue liquid in the zinc half-cell** — ZnSO₄ is colourless, and this is the
  single most common wrong image
- the blue of the copper sulphate **deepening** rather than paling
- **gas bubbles** at either electrode — a Daniell cell evolves no gas, and
  bubbling is what a generator will reach for when asked to show "a reaction"
- electrons or arrows running **copper → zinc**
- the two solutions **touching, mixing or sharing one vessel**
- the salt bridge **missing**, or drawn dipping into only one vessel
- **flame, spark, glow, smoke or boiling** — nothing here is hot or luminous
- the electrodes **touching each other** inside a vessel
- a **shiny mirror-bright** deposit; real copper deposit is dull and rough

## For a close view of one electrode

When the shot is only the zinc rod and its solution — no beakers, no wire, no
salt bridge in frame — the same rules hold, plus:

- the liquid stays **clear and colourless**; it does not cloud, tint or froth
- material leaves the rod as **fine, slow, downward drift**, not as bubbles and
  not as glowing particles
- the rod's silhouette **narrows**; it never lengthens or straightens
