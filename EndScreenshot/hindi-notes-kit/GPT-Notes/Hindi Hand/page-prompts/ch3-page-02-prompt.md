# THE HAND YOU MUST COPY — study IMAGE 2 and reproduce these exact traits

This writer's hand is FAST, CONFIDENT, SLIGHTLY UNTIDY BUT FULLY READABLE Devanagari, written in a single medium-fine BLUE ballpoint. The defining quality is that **almost nothing is uniform** — yet every word is still legible. Reproduce each of the following traits deliberately:

## ⛔⛔ THE ONE FAILURE MODE THAT RUINS THIS PAGE — READ FIRST

The overwhelmingly likely mistake is writing a **NEAT, EVEN, FONT-LIKE Devanagari** — evenly-sized letters, perfectly level rows, one continuous ruler-straight head-line per word, and equal gaps between every word. That output is a HANDWRITING FONT, and it is COMPLETELY WRONG here, no matter how pretty it looks. IMAGE 2 is a person writing FAST, not a typeface.

Before you commit the image, check it against these five tests. If it passes any of them, it is WRONG and you must redraw it messier:
1. Do the words of a line sit on ONE clean level baseline? → WRONG. Words must visibly step UP and DOWN against each other by up to a quarter of a letter's height, and each row must WAVE and tilt along its own crooked path.
2. Is every head-line (shirorekha) one long, level, unbroken bar? → WRONG. Head-lines are SHORT, SLANTED, BROKEN segments at differing heights, with tails overshooting the last letter.
3. Are the gaps between words all roughly equal? → WRONG. Some words must nearly COLLIDE; elsewhere a gap must be two or three times wider than its neighbour.
4. Are all the letters the same height and width? → WRONG. Neighbouring letters must differ noticeably in height, width, thickness and lean — even inside one word.
5. Does the right-hand end of the rows form a tidy edge, or do rows end at a comfortable, planned place? → WRONG. Line-ends must be CROWDED and squeezed, some spilling further right than others, badly ragged.

This unevenness is the WHOLE POINT of the page. Push it further than feels comfortable — a real rushed hand is messier than you expect — while keeping every single word perfectly readable and correctly spelt.

**1. THE SHIROREKHA (the top head-line) IS THE SIGNATURE — get this right.**
* The head-line is NOT one long, level, printed bar — this is the single most common way to get the page wrong. It is drawn in SHORT SEGMENTS, usually one segment per two–three joined letters (so even ONE word normally carries two or three separate head-line segments), each drawn as its own quick stroke.
* Each segment SLANTS a little — most tilt gently UPWARD to the right, a few tilt down; neighbouring segments in the same line do NOT share one angle.
* Segments frequently BREAK between letters inside a word (a small visible gap), and often OVERSHOOT past the last letter of the word by a few millimetres, leaving a short tail of line hanging in the air.
* The head-line sits at slightly DIFFERENT HEIGHTS over different letters, so the letters look HUNG from a slightly crooked wire rather than printed under a straight rail.
* Its thickness varies — some segments are pressed dark, some are thin and pale.

**2. NO TWO LETTERS SIT ON ONE BASELINE — there is NO shared baseline anywhere on this page.**
* Within a single word, individual letters ride HIGHER or LOWER than their neighbours and are TALLER or SHORTER — differences of roughly 15–25% in height, plainly visible at a glance.
* Within a line, whole words STEP UP AND DOWN against each other: a word may sit clearly above the word before it and the next one drop back down. Reading across a row, the writing should visibly WAVE.
* Each WHOLE LINE drifts along its own crooked imaginary baseline — usually starting near the left margin then sagging or climbing toward the right — and every line takes a DIFFERENT angle from the line above. Some rows tilt up, some tilt down. Never draw two consecutive rows at the same angle.
* Line-to-line VERTICAL SPACING is also uneven: some rows sit closer to the row above than others, and the gap may narrow or widen along the same row.
* The net effect: the page still reads as tidy rows from a distance, but nothing on it is actually straight or aligned.

**3. MATRAS AND VOWEL SIGNS ARE IRREGULAR.**
* The ि hook varies a lot in height and curl — sometimes it rises well above the head-line, sometimes it is a short stub; its top can lean left or right.
* The ी, े, ै, ो, ौ strokes are quick slanted TICKS of varying length and angle, sometimes detached from the head-line by a hair, sometimes crossing it.
* The anusvāra/chandrabindu dot (ं, ँ) is a small quick blob placed at slightly different heights, occasionally a touch off-centre over its letter.
* Halant/conjunct clusters (क्ष, त्र, ज्ञ, न्यू, क्लि, स्त) are written fast and compressed, the lower member squeezed under the upper — compact but still identifiable.

**4. WORD SPACING AND LINE-END BEHAVIOUR ARE UNEVEN.**
* Gaps between words vary WIDELY on the same line — this is very visible in IMAGE 2. Some words nearly TOUCH each other (the head-line of one almost running into the next), while elsewhere on the SAME row a gap is two or three times wider. ⛔ Never space words evenly; ⛔ never stretch a row so its words spread out neatly to the right edge (no justification of any kind).
* Toward the END of a line the writer CROWDS the letters, squeezing the last word or two narrower to make it fit, and occasionally lets the final letter or the danda spill slightly past where the previous lines ended. The right edge of the text is therefore strongly RAGGED and a little messy — never a justified block.
* Every line starts just to the RIGHT of the left double margin rule, but the starting x wanders by a few millimetres line to line; the odd letter may lean back onto the rule.

**5. SLANT.**
* The Devanagari is broadly UPRIGHT with a slight forward (rightward) lean, but the lean is not consistent — some letters stand vertical, some tilt forward, a few tip back. Verticals are drawn a bit off-plumb.

**6. ENGLISH / LATIN WORDS ARE WRITTEN IN A DIFFERENT SUB-HAND (very characteristic).**
* Wherever an English term appears (usually inside parentheses, e.g. "(Genetic Material)", "(Replication)", "(E. coli)"), it is written SMALLER than the surrounding Devanagari, in a JOINED, ROUNDED CURSIVE that leans clearly to the RIGHT — a visibly different texture from the upright Devanagari around it.
* These Latin words are often cramped, with the letters of a two-word term nearly running together; ascenders (l, t, b, d) are looped and the t-bars are quick short slashes.
* Digits are ordinary Latin numerals, written small and slightly rounded. Superscripts (10⁶, 10⁹, m⁻²) are drawn distinctly SMALLER and RAISED clear of the baseline.

**7. THE PEN.**
* ONE ink for the WHOLE page: a MUTED, GREYED, DARK BALLPOINT BLUE — never bright royal blue, never electric, never navy-black. Everything is this same blue: the title, every heading, all body text, every bullet dot, every box and arrow. ⛔ Do NOT introduce black, red, green or any second colour anywhere. ⛔ No highlighter, no colour fills, no shading.
* Ink darkness VARIES: down-strokes darker, quick strokes paler; whole words differ in shade; there are occasional dry-pen skips where the ball missed, and small dark pools where the pen paused or the writer RETRACED a stroke (retraced strokes look doubled/thickened — include a few).

**8. THE OVERALL IMPRESSION.**
* It must read as a real student writing at speed in a real notebook: organic, slightly crooked, individually imperfect letters, uneven spacing — but every single word clearly readable. "Irregular" NEVER means garbled, misspelt, smudged or sloppy.

⚠️ HARD RULE — HANDWRITTEN STROKE, NOT A FONT (NON-NEGOTIABLE): every occurrence of the SAME Devanagari letter must be formed a LITTLE differently each time — different width, tilt, height, curl and head-line segment. If any two instances of the same letter look pixel-identical, the page is WRONG. Never stamp a repeated glyph; never produce clean vector curves; loops need not close perfectly.
