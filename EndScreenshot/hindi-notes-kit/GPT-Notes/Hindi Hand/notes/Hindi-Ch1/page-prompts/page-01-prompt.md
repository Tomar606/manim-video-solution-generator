## ⛔⛔ THE ONE FAILURE MODE THAT RUINS THE PAGE

The likely mistake is **NEAT, EVEN, FONT-LIKE Devanagari** — equal letters, level rows, one ruler-straight head-line per word, equal word gaps. That is a HANDWRITING FONT and it is COMPLETELY WRONG, however pretty. IMAGE 2 is a person writing FAST, not a typeface. Check the finished page against these; passing ANY of them means redraw:

0a. Did you enlarge the writing because the page was short? → WRONG. Letter size is identical on every page of the chapter.
0b. Fewer than ~28 rows of writing down a full sheet? → WRONG: letters too big or rows too far apart.
1. Do a line's words sit on ONE clean baseline? → WRONG. Words step UP and DOWN by up to a quarter of a letter height; each row waves and tilts along its own crooked path.
2. Is each head-line (shirorekha) one long level bar? → WRONG. Head-lines are SHORT, SLANTED, BROKEN segments at differing heights, tails overshooting the last letter.
3. Are word gaps roughly equal? → WRONG. Some words nearly COLLIDE; elsewhere a gap is two or three times wider.
4. Are letters the same height and width? → WRONG. Neighbouring letters differ visibly in height, width, thickness and lean, even inside one word.
5. Do the rows end at a tidy right edge? → WRONG. Line-ends are CROWDED and squeezed, some spilling further right than others, badly ragged.

Push the unevenness further than feels comfortable — a real rushed hand is messier than you expect — while every word stays perfectly readable and correctly spelt.

**THE SHIROREKHA IS THE SIGNATURE.** Drawn in SHORT SEGMENTS, usually one per two–three joined letters (one word normally carries two or three separate segments). Each SLANTS a little, most tilting up to the right, neighbouring segments never sharing an angle. Segments BREAK between letters inside a word and often OVERSHOOT the last letter, leaving a short tail in the air. They sit at slightly different heights, so letters hang from a crooked wire, not a printed rail. Thickness varies — some pressed dark, some thin and pale.

**NO SHARED BASELINE ANYWHERE.** Inside one word letters ride higher or lower and are taller or shorter by 15–25%, plainly visible. Across a line whole words step up and down, so reading a row the writing WAVES. Each line drifts along its own crooked path — usually starting near the margin then sagging or climbing right — and every line takes a DIFFERENT angle from the one above; never two consecutive rows at the same angle. Row-to-row spacing is uneven too. From a distance it reads as tidy rows; nothing on it is actually straight.

**MATRAS ARE IRREGULAR.** The ि hook varies in height and curl, sometimes rising above the head-line, sometimes a stub, its top leaning either way. ी े ै ो ौ are quick slanted TICKS of varying length and angle, sometimes detached by a hair, sometimes crossing the head-line. The ं / ँ dot is a quick blob at slightly varying heights, occasionally a touch off-centre. Conjuncts (क्ष त्र ज्ञ न्यू क्लि स्त) are fast and compressed, the lower member squeezed under the upper — compact but identifiable.

**SPACING AND LINE-ENDS.** Word gaps vary WIDELY on the same line: some words nearly touch, elsewhere a gap is two or three times wider. ⛔ Never even spacing; ⛔ never justify a row out to the right edge. Toward a line's end the writer CROWDS letters, squeezing the last word narrower to fit, occasionally spilling a final letter or danda past the previous lines. Every line starts just right of the left double rule, but the starting x wanders a few millimetres line to line.

**SLANT.** Broadly upright with a slight rightward lean, inconsistently applied — some letters vertical, some tilted forward, a few tipped back. Verticals a bit off-plumb.

**LATIN WORDS ARE A DIFFERENT SUB-HAND.** English terms (usually in parentheses — "(Genetic Material)", "(E. coli)") are SMALLER than the Devanagari, joined, rounded, leaning clearly right — a visibly different texture. Often cramped; ascenders looped; t-bars quick slashes. Digits small and slightly rounded; superscripts (10⁶, m⁻²) distinctly smaller and raised.

⚠️ HARD RULE — STROKE, NOT FONT: every occurrence of the SAME Devanagari letter is formed a little differently — different width, tilt, height, curl, head-line segment.
