# GEO-C2-LA-01 — storyboard (rough sketch, awaiting approval)

**Question:** विश्व में जनसंख्या के वितरण और घनत्व को प्रभावित करने वाले कारकों की विवेचना कीजिए। (2024)
**Clips:** three parts, three reels — 2:22, 1:48, 2:29 (6:38 in all). Script and voice are fixed.
**Status:** APPROVED by the user 2026-10-07, with three notes: follow the script (outline only the three regions he names); the night globe is illustrative; pin the three religious places on the globe with the avatar hidden, and animate the avatar smoothly.

---

## 1. The direction

**"पृथ्वी, रात में" — the Earth seen at night from space.**

The question is literally *where people are and where they are not*. The most
honest picture of that is the planet at night: every light is people. The
Ganga plain glows, the Sahara and Tibet are black, the Nile is one bright
thread through darkness. So this video's home screen is a dark, turning globe
with real night lights, and we keep coming back to it. Each factor then
answers the question the globe asks: *why is it bright here and dark there?*

Where Magadha was warm, wooden and ancient, this one is dark, glowing and
modern: a view from a satellite.

**Why this helps the student:** the globe is the proof for every factor (lights
follow rivers; lights avoid deserts and mountains), and the answer's last
example — the three most crowded regions — is something he will have *seen
glowing* before he is asked to remember it.

## 2. The two threads that hold it together

1. **The night globe.** Opens every part, proves the key lines, carries the
   final example.
2. **The four-part ring.** A ring around the presenter with four segments —
   भौगोलिक · आर्थिक · सामाजिक एवं सांस्कृतिक · राजनीतिक. One segment lights each
   time a factor is finished, across all three parts. At the very end the ring
   opens into a full-screen wheel for the recap. It is the student's map of the
   answer: four parts, always visible, filling up.

```
│              │      │  │ write  │  │       │    only)     │
│              │      │  └────────┘  │       │              │
│ ╭──╮         │      │    ┌────┐    │       │              │
│ │😀│ ← in a  │      │    │ 😀 │    │       │              │
│ ╰──╯  ring   │      │   /│big │\   │       │  caption     │
│ caption      │      │    caption   │       │              │
└──────────────┘      └──────────────┘       └──────────────┘
 small circle,          large, centre,          hidden
 bottom-left,           dark background
 inside the 4-part ring
```

- **Showing** — he is a small circle bottom-left, inside the ring, and the
  picture has the whole frame. New for this video.
- **Telling** — when he says "exam में ज़रूर लिखना" or gives the answer's first
  line, he comes forward, large and centred, and the sentence to write appears
  as a card above him. The change of mode itself tells the student: *this one
  goes on paper.*
- **Map** — on the globe sequences he is off screen and his voice carries it.

## 10. Things to know before approving

1. **The script names "पश्चिमी यूरोप"; NCERT says north-western Europe, and
   also lists EAST Asia** among the densest regions. The script cannot change,
   so the globe will outline only the three regions he names — but on a real
   night map China and Japan glow just as brightly. Either leave them glowing
   and unnamed (honest, and what I would do), or dim them (tidier, less true).
2. **NCERT groups the factors as three** (political sits inside social and
   cultural); the script and the Notes answer use four. The video follows the
   script.
3. **Mecca, Varanasi and Prayagraj are real places.** They appear as named pins
   on the globe; the pilgrim clip is deliberately a general scene, not a
   generated picture of any one of them.
4. **Three reels, 6:38 in all, 18 Veo clips.** All three parts are designed
   together so the ring carries across them.
5. **Night lights are not a population count.** They also follow electricity
   and wealth, so a few crowded but poorer regions look dimmer than they are.
   The globe is therefore shown as "the Earth at night", never labelled as a
   density map, and it is only used for points it shows truthfully: lights
   along rivers, darkness over deserts, mountains and ice, and the three
   regions he names.
6. **The inbox folder's name ends in a space** (`GEO-C2-LA-01 `). I will rename
   it without the space when the build starts.
