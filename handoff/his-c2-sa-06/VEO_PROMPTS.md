# Notes for you — do not paste

## The one thing that decides whether this works

**Attach the same reference photograph of him as the first frame of every clip.**

A generated likeness of a real person drifts between generations — a slightly
different nose, a beard a shade lighter, a collar sitting differently. On a
stranger nobody notices. On *your own teacher*, whose face the student has seen
in twenty videos, every drift reads as wrong, and five clips will look like
five brothers. Words in a prompt cannot hold a face; a reference image can.

Use one clean, well-lit, front-facing frame of him — a still pulled from an
existing video is ideal, since it is exactly the person the students know.

## The audio, now that it is settled

**These five clips keep their own generated audio.** He speaks inside them. The
data screens — the question card, the three maps, the answer screen — use the
HeyGen clip instead, keyed in below the map, with HeyGen's picture and audio.

So the video alternates between two recordings of the same man's voice: Veo's
inside these clips, HeyGen's on the data screens. That is a real risk and it is
worth hearing before you commit to all five.

Generate clip 1, then play it straight after a HeyGen take of the same script.
If the two sound like the same person, carry on. If they do not, the fix is
already in your hands: the HeyGen script in this folder contains **every line in
the video, including these five**, so you can lay the HeyGen audio over the Veo
picture and discard Veo's sound. You keep the performance and the place, and one
voice carries the whole video.

That is why the HeyGen script is longer than the data screens need.

five clips.

fumble. If the pronunciation is wrong, that is an argument for option 1 above.
