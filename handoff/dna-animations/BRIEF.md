# Three DNA animations — production brief

Three short animated clips for a Class 12 MP Board revision video on **DNA
replication** (question: *"DNA प्रतिकृति क्या है? इसकी विधि लिखिए।"*, 2019,
4 marks). The video is finished and delivered; these clips drop into three
windows that are currently empty.

Everything needed is in this folder:

```
BRIEF.md                        this file
EXAMPLE_accepted_clip.mp4       a clip already accepted — match this look
references/ref_supercoiling.png the first frame for clip 1
references/ref_primer.png       the first frame for clip 2
references/ref_ligase.png       the first frame for clip 3
```

**Watch `EXAMPLE_accepted_clip.mp4` before starting.** It is the fourth clip in
this set, already approved, and it defines the palette, the materials, the
lighting and the background. The three new clips must look like they came off
the same machine on the same afternoon — they play in the same video, minutes
apart, and any drift in colour or material reads as a mistake.

---

## The rules, all three clips

These are not stylistic preferences. Each one comes from a clip that had to be
thrown away.

| # | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Not one character anywhere.** No letters, numerals, labels, captions, subtitles, watermarks or logos, in any script, at any moment. | Every label in this video is Devanagari and is typeset separately in the correct font and laid over the clip. A generated letterform is a defect even when it is beautiful. |
| 2 | **Start from the supplied reference image.** It is the clip's FIRST FRAME. Same parts, same number of them, same colours, same materials, same background. | The reference exists because words could not pin the structure down: earlier attempts grew a third backbone and rungs that floated free. |
| 3 | **16:9 landscape, 1920×1080 or 1280×720, 8–10 seconds, silent.** No audio track at all. | The clip is laid into a 16:9 panel. Sound would collide with the teacher's narration. |
| 4 | **Locked static camera.** No pan, push, zoom, orbit, handheld drift or rack focus. | The clip sits in a fixed panel; a moving camera makes the panel look like it is sliding. |
| 5 | **Background: soft, dark, out-of-focus depth.** Nothing in it sharp, nothing identifiable — no leaf, wall, bench, room, cell or blurred copy of the subject. Same from first frame to last. | It sits on a dark teal plate. A pale or busy background reads as a lightbox cut into the video. |
| 6 | **Colour identity survives the whole clip.** A part that starts amber ends amber; a part that starts slate blue ends slate blue. When one thing separates into two, they do NOT drift towards each other's colour. | Seen three times: the blue backbone drifted to tan partway through and both separated strands ended the same colour, so they could no longer be told apart. |
| 7 | **The subject is complete and centred**, both ends inside frame, an even margin of background on all four sides. Nothing cut off by an edge. It never grows, drifts or changes size. | The panel shows the whole frame; anything touching an edge looks amputated. |
| 8 | **The change finishes inside the clip and then HOLDS.** It runs once, comes to rest, and the final state stays still and unchanged to the last frame. | The last frame is held on screen to fill the narration. A clip still moving at the end cannot be held; one that reverses or restarts teaches the process backwards. |
| 9 | **Nothing decorative.** No sparkles, glints, lens flares, floating particles, dust motes, light rays, borders, vignettes, title cards or arrows that are not part of the mechanism. | A student who cannot say what an element is teaching them should not be seeing it. |
| 10 | **Nothing breaks, snaps or floats free** unless the brief says so. No gaps in a backbone, no detached pieces drifting. | Repeatedly produced by accident, and it teaches DNA damage rather than the process. |

## Clip 1 — Supercoiling and its release

- **File name:** `part1_at045.mp4`
- **Length:** 8–10 seconds (it will be fitted to a 9.6s window)
- **Reference / first frame:** `references/ref_supercoiling.png`

**What the teacher is saying over it**

> यहाँ पर, प्रतिकृति द्विशाख के आगे बन रहे अतिकुण्डलन, यानी supercoiling को दूर
> करने हेतु, टोपोआइसोमरेज एंजाइम, इसे अनेक स्थानों पर काट कर पुनः जोड़ देता है।

*(Supercoiling builds up ahead of the replication fork; the enzyme
topoisomerase relieves it.)*

**What must happen**

A wound DNA double helix, lying horizontally, evenly coiled along a straight
axis. It twists tighter and tighter under strain until **about two** of its
turns cross over themselves into coiled loops. Then the loops open out and the
helix settles back to evenly spaced turns along a straight axis — the way it
started.

**Must be true**

- Exactly two backbones throughout, one warm amber and one cool slate blue.
- It begins RELAXED — evenly spaced turns, nothing bunched, nothing crossed.
- The tightened, crossed-over state exists only in the MIDDLE of the clip.
- At its tightest the crossings stay open enough that each backbone can still be
  followed separately through them — no untraceable tangle.
- The last frame matches the first: straight axis, evenly spaced turns.
- Neither backbone is ever cut, nicked or broken.

> **Note:** an earlier version of this clip also asked for a Y-shaped fork to be
> held on screen while this happened. That was dropped — it failed three times.
> **Do not add a fork.** Just the helix, tightening and relaxing.

## Clip 2 — The RNA primer

- **File name:** `part2_at012.mp4`
- **Length:** 8–10 seconds (fitted to an 8.3s window)
- **Reference / first frame:** `references/ref_primer.png`

**What the teacher is saying over it**

> इसी से DNA अणु का नव संश्लेषण प्रारम्भ होता है।

*(This is what synthesis of the new DNA strand begins from.)*

**What must happen**

Starting from the supplied fork: a closed double helix on the right, two
separated single strands curving apart on the left, each carrying its own row of
half-rungs. A **short separate piece of nucleic acid, in a distinct warm green**,
settles down onto one of the separated strands and lies flat against it, matched
to a run of that strand's half-rungs. From the end of that green piece, a new
strand begins to extend along the template.

**Must be true**

- The green piece is visibly a different molecule from both backbones — it must
  never be mistaken for a third strand of the original helix.
- It lands ON the template strand and stays there; it does not drift off.
- The new strand grows FROM the end of the green piece, in one direction only.
- The fork holds its position and shape throughout; the separated strands do not
  zip back together.
- At the end the growth has stopped and the arrangement holds, still.

## Clip 3 — DNA ligase sealing a nick

- **File name:** `part3_at019.mp4`
- **Length:** 8–10 seconds (fitted to an 11.3s window)
- **Reference / first frame:** `references/ref_ligase.png`

**What the teacher is saying over it**

> छोटे-छोटे ओकाजाकी खण्ड, DNA लाइगेज की सहायता से जुड़ जाते हैं। मतलब Lagging
> strand पर बने छोटे-छोटे Okazaki fragments को, DNA ligase आपस में जोड़ता है।

*(The short Okazaki fragments are joined together by DNA ligase.)*

**What must happen**

Two short lengths of a single amber strand lie end to end along one line, their
facing ends stopping just short of each other with a small clean gap between
them. The two ends are drawn together until they meet, and the join seals — from
then on it is **one continuous unbroken strand**, with no visible seam.

**Must be true**

- Only two pieces. No third fragment appears.
- Both keep their row of half-rungs on the same side throughout.
- They move along their shared line towards each other; they do not swing,
  rotate or overlap.
- The final frame shows one continuous strand and holds there, still.
- No enzyme blob, machine or protein shape is drawn at the join — the sealing is
  shown by the gap closing, nothing else.

## Delivering them

Name the files exactly as above and send all three together:

```
part1_at045.mp4
part2_at012.mp4
part3_at019.mp4
```

**Format:** 16:9, 1920×1080 or 1280×720, MP4 (H.264), no audio track, 8–10s each.

Do not add fades, titles, borders or a watermark of any kind. Do not crop or
letterbox — deliver the full frame as rendered.

If a clip cannot satisfy one of the "must be true" points, send it anyway with a
note about which one — a clip that is right in every other respect may still be
usable, and knowing what is wrong is worth more than a second attempt at
guessing.
