# Voice card — the house style on one page

The short form of `script_index/RULES.md`, for the writer and the reviewers. Reading
this instead of the 130 KB INDEX.md is what keeps a review under two minutes.

## Direct and compact — the user's review of the first generated script
The first script came back with three notes: too long and too much explanation, too
many casual hooks, an indirect intro, and equations spelled out bracket by bracket.
Measured against the corpus it ran 907 words / 14 hook lines / 6 intro lines, where a
Short Answer runs **444 words / 6 hook lines / 2 intro lines**. So:

- **Intro = two lines.** The board line, then one line that names what is asked and
  goes straight into the first item. No term definitions, no "पहले ये समझ लो", no
  "तो चलो…" before teaching starts.
  `MP Board कक्षा 12वीं के बच्चों! ये सवाल 2024 में board exam में पूछा जा चुका है, और इस बार भी आपकी परीक्षा में आ सकता है।`
  `तो सबसे पहले बात करते हैं <first item> की।`
- **Explanation budget: book line + ONE gloss.** Add a reason only where the question
  asks why. Don't define a term the student met in class (लिगैण्ड, समावयवी); don't
  explain the notation; don't add a contrast, a recipe line and a memory line — pick
  at most one.
- **Hooks go in the MIDDLE, not at the seams.** About 6–8 hook lines in a Short Answer,
  counting the fixed opening and close. The intro and the close stay lean; the hooks
  that make a script catchy sit *inside* the explanation — see "Hooks in the middle"
  below. (Removing all of them was the second review's complaint: the script went flat.)
- **Length: Short Answer ≈ 450 words total.** A split into two parts does not buy
  twice the words.
- **A clarity fix replaces, it never adds.** If a line confuses, rewrite that line.

## Spoken words, not written ones
The presenter talks; he does not read out a textbook. Measured on the 111 scripts:

| Never say | Say | Corpus, in talking lines | Even inside textbook sentences |
|---|---|---|---|
| यह | **ये** | 76% ये | ये used about half the time |
| वह | **वो** | 82% वो | |
| तथा / एवं | **और** | 98% और | और 392 : तथा 19 |
| किन्तु / परन्तु | **लेकिन / पर** | 99% लेकिन | लेकिन 12 : किन्तु 0 |
| अतः | **इसलिए** | 98% | |
| अर्थात् | **यानी** | 97% | |
| केवल | **सिर्फ** | | |
| इस प्रकार | **ऐसे / इस तरह** | 97% | |

**The rule: technical terms stay exactly as the book writes them; connectors are
always spoken ones — including inside the textbook sentence.** "यह उप-सहसंयोजी मण्डल
के अन्दर तथा बाहर के लिगैण्ड के विनिमय के कारण उत्पन्न होती है" is said as "ये
उप-सहसंयोजी मण्डल के अंदर और बाहर के लिगैण्ड के विनिमय के कारण होती है". Every mark
lives in the terms (उप-सहसंयोजी मण्डल, विनिमय), not in यह/तथा.

## Hooks in the middle — how the corpus keeps it catchy
Hooks are spread evenly through a script (about a fifth in every fifth of it), and a
Short Answer carries **2–5 inside the explanation** (median 2, p75 5). What makes them
feel natural rather than bolted on:

1. **Right before the point they flag** — the trap, the key line, the number to
   remember. Never floating, never at the end of a point.
2. **They name the thing**: "अब ये ध्यान से समझो कि भ्रूण कोष 7 कोशिकीय और 8
   केन्द्रकीय क्यों होता है।" beats a bare "ध्यान दो".
3. **Five kinds, varied — never the same wording twice:**
   - attention: `अब ध्यान दो, …` · `अब यहाँ थोड़ा ध्यान देना, …`
   - importance: `अब सबसे ज़रूरी बात—` · `ये बहुत important है, क्योंकि …`
   - trap: `यहाँ बच्चे अक्सर गलती करते हैं—…` · `यहीं पर सबसे ज्यादा बच्चे उलझते हैं।`
   - curiosity: `और अब बीच में क्या है?` · `तो फिर फर्क कहाँ है?`
   - lock-in, right after a key fact: `बस ये एकदम पक्का कर लो—7 cells, 8 nuclei।`
4. **One hook, then the point.** Two hooks back to back is filler — and a curiosity
   question followed by "अब ध्यान दो" in the same line counts as two.
6. **Vary the KIND, not just the words.** Three hooks that all say ध्यान (सुनना / दो /
   समझना) read as one hook repeated. Use at least two kinds, and at least one importance
   or trap hook ("ये बहुत important है, क्योंकि …", "यहीं पर बच्चे अक्सर उलझते हैं कि …").
7. **A hook that names its point is a claim — it must be true.** "पूरा फर्क सिर्फ एक
   atom का है" was false (isomers contain the same atoms; only which atom bonds differs)
   and a student would have written it down.
8. **Put a trap hook right before the line that resolves the trap** — not before a
   rephrase of the definition, and never between a line and its "यानी / मतलब" gloss.
5. **Spoken register**, like everything else: ये, वो, लेकिन.

## Fixed lines — use as written
- **Open (part 1, first line):** `MP Board कक्षा 12वीं के बच्चों! ये सवाल <वर्ष> में board exam में पूछा जा चुका है, और इस बार भी आपकी परीक्षा में आ सकता है।`
- **Close (final part only, in order):**
  1. `तो बच्चों, <topic> वाला सवाल भी पूरा हो गया।`
  2. `अब इसका पूरा answer परीक्षा में कैसे लिखना है, वो आपकी स्क्रीन के right side में दिए Notes button पर मिल जाएगा।`
  3. `अब आगे की पढ़ाई वाली reels देखते जाओ और एक-एक करके पूरा syllabus complete करते जाओ।`
- **Part 2+ opens:** `बच्चों, Part 1 में हमने <the actual result, named> समझा था।` — name the result, so a cold viewer has context.
- **Non-final part ends:** `<X> हो गया, लेकिन <Y> अभी बाकी है। … इसलिए Part N ज़रूर देखना।`

## The teaching unit
1. announce the item — `सबसे पहले बात करते हैं <X> की।` / `अब दूसरा …`
2. optional dictation cue **with a reason**, once per script at most — `ध्यान से सुनना, क्योंकि answer की पहली line यही बनेगी।`
3. **the book sentence, verbatim** — what the student writes
4. **the gloss**, shorter than the book line — `मतलब …` / `यानी …`
5. **why** — क्योंकि / इसलिए
6. where the point is a trap or a must-write line, a hook right BEFORE it (see "Hooks in the middle"), and at most one lock-in line after it

## Register
- तुम-form teacher (समझ लो, याद रखना, लिखना). `आपकी` only inside the two fixed lines.
- Short lines: median 12 words, rarely over 26. One idea per line.
- About one line in four is about the exam/answer/marks — no more.
- English discourse words in Latin script: *important, definition, answer, exam, point, step, line*.
  Subject terms Hindi first, English after यानी: `उभयदन्ती लिगैण्ड, यानी ambidentate ligand`.
- Never: मार्क्स (say नंबर), फॉलो, वेलकम बैक, वापस आ गए, सेट है, दोस्तों, romanised Hindi,
  the generic "बहुत सारे बच्चे यहीं गलती करते हैं" (name the actual confusion instead),
  invented statistics ("सौ में से अस्सी").
- Don't open two seams the same way (three "तो बच्चों," in a row is a defect).

## Anything spoken must be speakable
HeyGen voices the line exactly as written.
- `Ni²⁺` → `Ni two plus` · `संरचना-I` → `संरचना एक`
- **Chemistry: describe what the equation says, in Hindi — never spell it out.** The
  equation is on screen; the voice says what happens:
  `इस अभिक्रिया में लेड, सल्फेट आयन से मिलकर PbSO₄ बनाता है और दो इलेक्ट्रॉन बाहर निकल जाते हैं।` (CHE-C2-SA-07)
  For a complex, name its parts: `इसमें Co के साथ पाँच अमोनिया और ब्रोमाइड अंदर हैं, और सल्फेट बाहर।`
  **Never** `open bracket Co, open bracket N H three close bracket five…` — only the formal
  C4/C5 Short Answer batch does that (11 scripts), and the user rejected it in review.
- Physics: the relation in words — `E बराबर है q by four pi epsilon naught r square`.
- Pick ONE read-out dialect per script.

## Screen text
- Organises; never retypes the spoken sentence. 3–6 words per node.
- An arrow means a reaction or a flow. **Labels use a colon**: `I : [Ni(NH₃)₅(SCN)]Cl`, never `I → …`.
- A dissociation on screen must balance: `[Co(NH₃)₅Br]SO₄ → [Co(NH₃)₅Br]²⁺ + SO₄²⁻`.
  Or, simpler and closer to the Notes wording, use a label: `[Co(NH₃)₅Br]SO₄ : SO₄²⁻ देता है`.
  Never the half-way form `→ SO₄²⁻`, which is an unbalanced reaction a student will copy.
- Every on-screen formula also gets a spoken read-out.

## Before the first draft — the mistakes round 1 keeps catching
- **Define a term only if the book line cannot be followed without it** and it is not something taught in class. Most scripts define nothing beyond what the question asks.
- **Connect the example to the definition**: say which part of the example is the thing just defined.
- **State as few rules as possible**; if you state one, give its reason in the same line.
- **One word per thing**: don't call the same species a लिगैण्ड in one line and an आयन in the next without saying why.
- **Check the source answer's terms**, don't just copy them: the source has been wrong (मूलानुपाती for अणुसूत्र, garbled spellings, duplicate lines). Flag every correction to the user.
- **Tense**: two separate compounds are described in the present, not as one turning into the other.
- **Pronouns must point at one thing**: "…यौगिक…, और इसी को समावयवता कहते हैं" makes the compounds the phenomenon. Name it: "और इस घटना को …".
- **Don't start two adjacent lines the same way** (अब … / अब …), and don't let यानी become the new मतलब tic — vary with a plain restatement.
- **Screen labels in Hindi word order**: `[Co(NH₃)₅Br]SO₄ : SO₄²⁻ देता है`, not `: देता है SO₄²⁻`.
- **The memory line goes before the fixed close**, never between its lines.
- **Keep a Short Answer near 450 words in total**, whether or not it is split. The first generated script ran 907 against the human version's 583; the excess was clarifying lines a reviewer asked for. Prefer one precise sentence over two explanatory ones.
- **Length**: ~250–300 spoken words per part once split; ~450 total for a Short Answer.
